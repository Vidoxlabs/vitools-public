Internal Communications Skill

When to use

Activate on keywords: "status report", "update", "newsletter", "weekly update", "3P", "incident report", "FAQ", "leadership update", "project update", "comms".

Communication types

3P Update (Progress / Plans / Problems)

Format:





Progress: 3-5 bullets on what was accomplished. Be specific (metrics, deliverables, milestones).



Plans: 3-5 bullets on what comes next. Include timelines and owners.



Problems: Blockers, risks, asks. Be direct about what you need.

Tone: Concise, factual, action-oriented. No filler. Each bullet should be one sentence.

Status Report

Format:





Header: Project name, date, author, status (Green/Amber/Red)



Executive summary: 2-3 sentences max



Key metrics / KPIs: table format



Accomplishments: bullet list



Upcoming milestones: table with dates and owners



Risks & issues: table with severity, owner, mitigation



Decisions needed: numbered list

Leadership Update

Format:





TL;DR: 1-2 sentences — the one thing the exec should know



Context: Why this matters now



Key data: 3-5 metrics or facts



Recommendation: What you're proposing



Ask: What you need from them

Tone: Crisp, confident, opinionated. Executives want recommendations, not options.

Company Newsletter

Format:





Intro: 2-3 sentences setting the tone



Sections: Product updates, team highlights, upcoming events, fun facts



Each section: headline + 1-2 paragraphs + optional image placeholder

Tone: Warm, inclusive, celebratory but not cheesy.

Incident Report

Format:





Summary: What happened, when, impact scope



Timeline: Chronological events with timestamps



Root cause: Technical and process causes



Impact: Users/systems affected, duration, data implications



Resolution: What was done to fix it



Prevention: Action items to prevent recurrence

Tone: Factual, blameless, forward-looking.

General guidelines





Lead with the most important information



Use bullet points over paragraphs



Include specific numbers and dates, not vague language



End with clear action items or next steps



Match the tone to the audience (exec vs. team vs. company-wide)
