Document Review Skill

When to use

Activate on: "review this document", "check for compliance", "audit", "verify completeness", "gap analysis", "quality check", "is this complete", "what's missing".

Approach

Step 1: Understand the review criteria





What type of document is being reviewed? (contract, report, questionnaire, form)



What standards or checklist should it be reviewed against?



What are the critical vs. nice-to-have requirements?



Is there a template or reference document to compare against?

Step 2: Systematic review





Completeness check: Are all required sections/fields present?



Consistency check: Do different parts of the document agree with each other?



Accuracy check: Are numbers, dates, names, and references correct?



Compliance check: Does it meet the specified standards or rules?



Quality check: Is the language clear, unambiguous, and professional?

Step 3: Report findings

Structure the review output as:





Summary: overall assessment in 2-3 sentences



Critical Issues: blockers, compliance failures, missing required material



Important Gaps: inconsistencies, unsupported claims, unclear wording, weak evidence



Minor Suggestions: polish, formatting, or optional improvements



Questions: decisions or source material needed from the user



Recommended Next Steps: concrete edits or follow-up checks

For each finding, include:





Location: section, heading, page, paragraph, or quoted snippet when available



Severity: critical, important, or minor



Why it matters



Suggested fix

Quality standards





Do not invent compliance requirements. If the review standard is missing, ask for it or label your review as a general quality review.



Preserve the author's intent; focus on risks and improvements.



Distinguish confirmed issues from questions or assumptions.



When reviewing long documents, prioritize high-impact findings over exhaustive copy edits.
